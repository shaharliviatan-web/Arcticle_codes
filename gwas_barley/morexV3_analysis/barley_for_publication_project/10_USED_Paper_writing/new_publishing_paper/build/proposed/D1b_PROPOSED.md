**Reads after approval:**

> The trade-off also had a genomic counterpart. Fiber and starch loci co-localized in three regions, which resolved to a single gene on chromosome 7H, to two genes on 3H and to no gene with a known role in either trait on 1H. At 7H, a single haplotype of the gene carried both effects, higher fiber and lower starch of similar magnitude. With the two lead SNPs only 154 bp apart and no other annotated gene within 112 kb, this pattern is more simply explained by one causal variant expressed through two correlated traits than by two linked genes. Moreover, the carriers of the minor alleles at this locus, and of the haplotype carrying both effects, came from sites across the environmental gradient of the collection, so this shared signal cannot be explained as a geographic artifact of the accessions' origin. The gene encodes a GDSL esterase/lipase rather than a starch enzyme, so its link to starch is more likely compositional than mechanistic, as a shift in the cell-wall fraction changes the share of starch in the grain in the opposite direction. At 3H, the two genes were a cutin-biosynthetic acyltransferase (*GPAT6*) for fiber and a plastid phosphate transporter (*PHT4;3*) for starch. Thus, the carbon-allocation axis was visible at every level the data could resolve.
