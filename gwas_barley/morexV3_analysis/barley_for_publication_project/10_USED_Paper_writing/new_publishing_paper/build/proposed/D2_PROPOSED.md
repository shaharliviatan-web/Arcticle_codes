> Most associations rested on rare alleles, and at every fiber, β-glucan and starch locus the minor allele shifted grain composition in the direction of the phenotypic correlations, toward higher fiber and β-glucan and lower starch. This consistency may partly reflect where the carriers came from: at most fiber and β-glucan loci, they originated mainly from desert and coast–desert sites, the populations that already produce low-starch, fiber- and β-glucan-rich grain (Online Resource X<sup>(TODO 419)</sup>). Such regional enrichment is expected if these alleles contributed to local adaptation, and does not by itself indicate an artifact. The carriers also came from several desert sites rather than a single one, and at most loci they differed from non-carriers of their own site in the direction of the allelic effect, supporting a genetic effect beyond geographic origin. However, correction for population structure is less effective for rare alleles shared by related accessions, so a contribution of common ancestry cannot be fully excluded. Plausible gene functions further support individual loci. For the candidate genes, however, the allelic effect is harder to separate from geographic origin: the haplotypes associated with high fiber at *GPAT6* and *GH17* came mainly from several desert populations, and the high-starch haplotype of *PHT4;3* was nearly absent from them. The 7H locus, whose carriers and haplotypes were not concentrated in the desert, therefore provides the clearest case.
