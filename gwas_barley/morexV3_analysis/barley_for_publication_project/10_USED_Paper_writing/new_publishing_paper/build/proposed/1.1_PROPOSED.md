## Change 2 of 3 · Discussion §2 ¶2 ("The trade-off also had…"), sentences 2–3

*Why:* The direction of allelic effects moves to §3 ¶2, next to its caveat. The genomic counterpart now rests on the co-localization and the 7H haplotype. "Moreover" is dropped because it no longer follows a first point.

The trade-off also had a genomic counterpart. <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">Consistent with a shared axis, at every locus the minor allele shifted grain composition in the direction of the phenotypic correlations, toward higher fiber and β-glucan and lower starch. Moreover, fiber</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">Fiber</ins> and starch loci co-localized in three regions, and these resolved at three different levels. On chromosome 7H, the two loci resolved to a single gene, and a single haplotype of this gene carried both effects, higher fiber and lower starch of similar magnitude. With the two lead SNPs only 154 bp apart, this pattern is more simply explained by one causal variant expressed through two correlated traits than by two linked genes. Moreover, the carriers of the minor alleles at this locus were drawn from across the ecological range of the collection rather than concentrated in the desert populations <sup>(TODO 403)</sup>, so this shared signal cannot be attributed to the geographic origin of the accessions. The gene encodes a GDSL esterase/lipase rather than a starch enzyme, so its link to starch is more likely compositional than mechanistic, as a shift in the cell-wall fraction changes the share of starch in the grain in the opposite direction. On chromosome 3H, the two neighbouring loci resolved to two genes, one per trait, a cutin-biosynthetic acyltransferase for fiber and a plastid phosphate transporter for starch. On chromosome 1H, the region contained no gene with a known role in either trait. Thus, the carbon-allocation axis was visible at every level the data could resolve, and where a region could not be resolved, nothing contradicted it.

## Change 3 of 3 · Discussion §3 ¶2 ("Most associations rested…"), first sentence

*Why:* The direction is stated here once, right before its caveat. "Every fiber, β-glucan and starch locus" replaces the old "every locus", because the two protein loci have opposite effects (Results Ch. 2 ¶3).

Most associations rested on rare alleles, and <ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">at every fiber, β-glucan and starch locus the minor allele shifted grain composition in the direction of the phenotypic correlations, toward higher fiber and β-glucan and lower starch. This</ins><del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">their</del> consistent direction requires closer attention. At most fiber and β-glucan loci, the minor-allele carriers originated mainly from desert and coast–desert sites <sup>(TODO 405)</sup>, the populations that already produce low-starch, fiber- and β-glucan-rich grain. Such regional enrichment is expected if these alleles contributed to local adaptation, and does not by itself indicate an artefact. The carriers also came from several desert sites rather than a single one, and they differed from non-carriers of their own site in the direction of the allelic effect, supporting a genetic effect beyond geographic origin. However, the desert populations are closely related to one another, and correction for population structure is less effective for rare alleles shared by related accessions, so a contribution of common ancestry cannot be fully excluded. Plausible gene functions and phenotype-associated haplotypes further support individual loci, and the 7H locus, whose carriers were not concentrated in the desert, provides the clearest case.
