## Change 2 of 9 · Results Ch. 1 ¶2 ("To examine whether variation…"), Sariel's text (8.2)

**With the changes marked:**

To examine whether variation in grain nutritional traits followed the environmental gradient of the collection sites, trait BLUPs were first visualized across the 29 sites ordered by ecological region (Fig. 2a). This descriptive pattern suggested that northern-derived genotypes generally had higher starch, whereas desert-derived genotypes tended to have lower starch and higher fiber and β-glucan; protein showed no clear directional trend. Among the nutritional traits, starch was strongly negatively correlated with fiber (r = −0.78; Fig. 2b) and β-glucan (r = −0.53), while fiber and β-glucan were positively correlated (r = 0.35; all <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">P</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">*P*</ins> \< 0.001). Protein was poorly correlated with other traits, and significant negative correlation was obtained only for protein-starch (r = <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">-0.12</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">−0.12</ins>, <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">P</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">*P*</ins> \< 0.05). Among the morphological and phenological traits, starch was strongly correlated with grain weight, plant height, and flowering time, while fiber showed the exact opposite pattern. ## Change 8 of 9 · Discussion §2 ¶2 ("The trade-off also had…") (8.1)

*Why:* US spelling.

**With the changes marked:**

The trade-off also had a genomic counterpart. Fiber and starch loci co-localized in three regions, and these resolved at three different levels. On chromosome 7H, the two loci resolved to a single gene, and a single haplotype of this gene carried both effects, higher fiber and lower starch of similar magnitude. With the two lead SNPs only 154 bp apart, this pattern is more simply explained by one causal variant expressed through two correlated traits than by two linked genes. Moreover, the carriers of the minor alleles at this locus were drawn from across the ecological range of the collection rather than concentrated in the desert populations <sup>(TODO 403)</sup>, so this shared signal cannot be attributed to the geographic origin of the accessions. The gene encodes a GDSL esterase/lipase rather than a starch enzyme, so its link to starch is more likely compositional than mechanistic, as a shift in the cell-wall fraction changes the share of starch in the grain in the opposite direction. On chromosome 3H, the two <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">neighbouring</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">neighboring</ins> loci resolved to two genes, one per trait, a cutin-biosynthetic acyltransferase (*GPAT6*) for fiber and a plastid phosphate transporter (*PHT4;3*) for starch. On chromosome 1H, the region contained no gene with a known role in either trait. Thus, the carbon-allocation axis was visible at every level the data could resolve.

## Change 9 of 9 · Discussion §3 ¶2 ("Most associations rested…") (8.1)

*Why:* US spelling.

**With the changes marked:**

Most associations rested on rare alleles, and at every fiber, β-glucan and starch locus the minor allele shifted grain composition in the direction of the phenotypic correlations, toward higher fiber and β-glucan and lower starch. This consistency may partly reflect where the carriers came from: at most fiber and β-glucan loci, they originated mainly from desert and coast–desert sites <sup>(TODO 405)</sup>, the populations that already produce low-starch, fiber- and β-glucan-rich grain. Such regional enrichment is expected if these alleles contributed to local adaptation, and does not by itself indicate an <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">artefact</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">artifact</ins>. The carriers also came from several desert sites rather than a single one, and they differed from non-carriers of their own site in the direction of the allelic effect, supporting a genetic effect beyond geographic origin. However, the desert populations are closely related to one another, and correction for population structure is less effective for rare alleles shared by related accessions, so a contribution of common ancestry cannot be fully excluded. Plausible gene functions and phenotype-associated haplotypes further support individual loci, and the 7H locus, whose carriers were not concentrated in the desert, provides the clearest case.
