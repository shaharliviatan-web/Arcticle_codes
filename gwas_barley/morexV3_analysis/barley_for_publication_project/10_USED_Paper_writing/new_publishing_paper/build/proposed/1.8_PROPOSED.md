## Change 2 of 2 · Results Ch. 3 ¶3, after the last sentence ("…are presented here (Online Resource 2).")

*Why:* The two top-ranked genes are kept as a bare fact, now after the presented genes: they were not among them, and why (no functional call; a transposase-derived domain). The interpretation (significance identifies the locus, function chooses the gene) stays in Discussion §3 ¶3.

The 23 significant genes were not 23 independent findings: they fell in only 12 loci, and genes within a locus lie in the same linkage-disequilibrium block. A single locus, the 1.73 Mb fiber locus on chromosome 3H, contained seven of the 12 significant fiber genes. Among the 23, statistical rank and biological plausibility pointed in different directions: the most significant gene of the analysis (*HORVU.MOREX.r3.6HG0619810*, starch, *q* = 5.5 × 10⁻¹¹) returned no functional call from Swiss-Prot, InterPro or NCBI nr, and the second (*HORVU.MOREX.r3.3HG0301210*, fiber, *q* = 1.1 × 10⁻⁹) carried a transposase-derived domain, so neither can be connected to its trait through its function. Genes were therefore carried forward in two stages: significance in the haplotype analysis was the gate, and among the genes that passed it a biological-relevance screen kept only those with a documented or plausible role in the biosynthesis, transport or regulation of the corresponding grain component, rather than those ranking highest by significance.
