## The proposed Conclusions (all of it is new, so all of it would be green)

<div style="border:2px solid #1e8449;padding:8px">

**Conclusions**

Grain composition in wild barley is organized around carbon allocation involving starch, fiber and β-glucan, alongside a distinct protein axis governed by carbon and nitrogen balance. By applying genome-wide association and haplotype analysis to ecologically diverse populations, this research identified heritable variation and specific genetic architectures for these traits. Mapping success did not follow heritability, most associations rested on rare alleles, and the strongest β-glucan signals lay away from the canonical glucan pathway. The starch–fiber trade-off recurred in the genome, most clearly at the shared 7H locus and at the neighboring fiber and starch loci on 3H, and four candidate genes link grain composition to cutin deposition, glucan hydrolysis, plastid phosphate transport and cell-wall ester modification.

These defined loci, together with the high-fiber wild haplotypes absent from the elite cultivars examined, provide precise targets for improving cultivated barley nutrition while minimizing the linkage drag commonly associated with wild germplasm introgression (Hübner and Kantar 2021). Previous research successfully transferred beneficial wild alleles to increase β-amylase activity and yield (citation) <sup>(TODO 411)</sup>, yet the simultaneous transfer of undesirable traits remains a persistent challenge. Refining broad genetic associations into a focused set of biologically plausible targets simplifies the monitoring of transferred genomic segments. Future breeding efforts must validate these haplotypes in elite cultivars, replicate their effects in independent populations, and conduct functional tests to assess their overall agronomic impact.
