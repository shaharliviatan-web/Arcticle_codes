**With the changes marked:**

The trade-off also had a genomic counterpart. Fiber and starch loci co-localized in three regions, and these resolved at three different levels. On chromosome 7H, the two loci resolved to a single gene, and a single haplotype of this gene carried both effects, higher fiber and lower starch of similar magnitude. With the two lead SNPs only 154 bp apart and no other annotated gene within 112 kb, this pattern is more simply explained by one causal variant expressed through two correlated traits than by two linked genes. Moreover, the carriers of the minor alleles at this locus, and of the haplotype carrying both effects, came from <del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">all environments</del><ins style="color:#1e8449;background:#e9f7ef;text-decoration:none">sites across the environmental gradient of the collection</ins>, so this shared signal cannot be explained as a geographic artifact of the accessions' origin. The gene encodes a GDSL esterase/lipase rather than a starch enzyme, so its link to starch is more likely compositional than mechanistic, as a shift in the cell-wall fraction changes the share of starch in the grain in the opposite direction. On chromosome 3H, the two neighboring loci resolved to two genes, one per trait, a cutin-biosynthetic acyltransferase (*GPAT6*) for fiber and a plastid phosphate transporter (*PHT4;3*) for starch. On chromosome 1H, the region contained no gene with a known role in either trait. Thus, the carbon-allocation axis was visible at every level the data could resolve.
