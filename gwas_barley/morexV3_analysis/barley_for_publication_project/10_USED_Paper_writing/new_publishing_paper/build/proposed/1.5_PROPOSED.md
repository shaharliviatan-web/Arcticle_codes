For starch, the plastid-localised anion/phosphate transporter *HORVU.MOREX.r3.3HG0301710* (3H:546,473,709–546,477,564) was tested on 205 of the 290 accessions, the only one of the three tested on a majority of the panel, and resolved into four haplotype groups (*q* = 3.8 × 10⁻⁴, η² = 0.078, 1.31 SD; Fig. 4c). It belongs to the PHT4 family and was identified as an ortholog of rice PHT4;3 at 84.2% identity over 99.8% of the protein, the best protein identification of the three. Phosphate is the allosteric inhibitor of ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase, the committed step of starch synthesis, so the rate at which phosphate crosses the plastid envelope constrains starch accumulation, and loss of the related plastidic transporter PHT4;2 alters starch accumulation in *Arabidopsis* by this route (Guo et al. 2008; Irigoyen et al. 2011) <sup>(TODO 307)</sup>. Its effect is the smallest of the three, and the documented starch phenotype belongs to PHT4;2 rather than to this paralog, so the mechanism is inferred at the level of the family. The gene lies 40 kb from its lead SNP, the closest of the three<del style="color:#c0392b;background:#fdecea">, and 1.47 Mb from the acyltransferase above, the two sitting in the fiber and starch loci separated by 595 kb on the long arm of chromosome 3H (Fig. 3b, d)</del>.
