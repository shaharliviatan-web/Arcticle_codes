**Reads after approval:**

> Grain composition in wild barley is organized around carbon allocation involving starch, fiber and β-glucan, alongside a distinct protein axis governed by carbon and nitrogen balance. By applying genome-wide association and haplotype analysis to ecologically diverse populations, this research identified heritable variation and specific genetic architectures for these traits. Trait associations varied significantly and did not follow heritability; starch, the most heritable trait, mapped to few loci, whereas fiber was distributed across the most. Most associations rested on rare alleles, and β-glucan produced the strongest of them yet contributed no candidate gene. The starch–fiber trade-off recurred in the genome, most clearly at the shared 7H locus and at the neighboring fiber and starch loci on 3H, and four candidate genes link grain composition to cutin deposition, glucan hydrolysis, plastid phosphate transport and cell-wall ester modification.
